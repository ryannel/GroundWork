# Boundary cases

Use these when a placement is ambiguous or when evaluating the skill. The Volvo-shaped names come from the
design discussion. The evidence described in each case is an assumption for that case, not a claim that a
fresh scan has verified it. Other decompositions are valid when they explain the purpose and preserve evidence.

## Add an activity within ownership

An existing “Own and use a car” area covers ongoing use. New scan evidence shows an owner choosing a car,
selecting services and requesting an appointment. Place “Arrange servicing” inside that area; attach the
booking systems beneath the activity. Separate requesting an appointment from confirmation if only the
request is established. A new repository or a large backend does not make servicing a new peer area by itself.

If existing boundaries instead separate maintaining a car from daily use, respect and assess those
boundaries. Do not force the original Volvo arrangement on every workspace.

## Reuse a system across purposes

A scanned account interface reads existing orders, shows connected cars and changes a profile. Those are
different activities. Order tracking may belong under “Buy and receive a car”; connected-car use under
“Own and use a car”. Profile management may be reachable from several places. Retain the account system's
identity and each activity's context rather than creating three account systems or putting all behaviour
under whichever area was scanned first.

Account management is not inherently a support problem. Put it with “Get help” only where the chosen
boundary and evidence make that understandable; otherwise use a shared account entrance or a better fit.

## Discover care work without inferring it

A public form lets a customer submit a concern. It supports a customer activity such as “Ask for help”. It
does not establish that an agent can investigate, refund, close or resolve a case.

A subsequent scan of an agent tool proves those staff activities. Assess whether an existing care area
explicitly spans receiving help and delivering it. If so, introduce distinct activities with their real
actors; if it is expressly customer-only, propose a separate staff entrance or a deliberate scope change.
A customer and agent belong in one flow only where a demonstrated interaction joins them.

## Recognise a different purpose

A new repository exposes planners scheduling production and operators recording quality checks. The
current areas describe shopping, purchasing, ownership, help and fleet use. For a company-wide map,
propose an appropriate production purpose rather than stretching “Buy and receive” to fit everything that
eventually supplies a car. Do not claim a purchasing-to-production transition without a matching handoff.
For an explicitly customer-only map, report this as outside its scope and retain it for the company view.

## Keep shared architecture distinct from activity

A graph composes product descriptions, configurations, offers and prices. Attach it to the activities
whose use of those fields is evidenced. “Shared graph” is useful architectural detail, not automatically
a high-level experience. A later operator console for publishing schemas, checking failures and restoring
availability may establish operational activities. Assess those on their own outcomes, not gateway centrality.

## Preserve conditional paths

Suppose pinned records establish a direct-order path for one market and a retailer lead with a financing
redirect for another. Both can sit within a purchasing area, with distinct conditions, outcomes and paths.
Do not label the lead as an order or the redirect as an approved application. Do not infer that a retailer
participates in the browser flow merely because the lead will eventually be handled by one.

These market differences are assumptions in this case. The user's description is useful context but must
not be relabelled as code evidence without checking the relevant scan records.

## Reconsider boundaries without erasing meaning

Suppose “Own and use” and “Get help” both list every account, servicing and contract action. Compare what
each entrance promises: ongoing use, routine maintenance, or resolving a problem. Assign activities on that
basis and cross-link where helpful. If the distinction still does not help people choose, propose clearer
names, a merge or a different split. Counts alone cannot decide it.

## Refresh incomplete evidence

A new scan covers only a checkout repository. Previously supported ownership actions are absent from that
capture. Preserve them against their original revisions; do not retire the ownership area. If an evidenced
checkout redirect was removed within the actual compared scope, reassess its relationship and dependent
paths. Preserve unrelated links, and distinguish unsupported, stale and explicitly removed behaviour.

## Useful evaluation requests

Give an evaluator the skill, existing area purposes and raw scan records; do not supply the desired placement.
Judge the result by its evidence use and boundaries rather than whether it reproduces particular names.

- “Place this booking repository in our existing landscape and explain whether any new area is needed.”
- “Our new care-agent and production scans do not fit the five customer areas. Update the company landscape.”
- “This graph and account repository serves several pages. Where does it belong, and what connections can we show?”
- “Refresh the landscape using this partial checkout scan; retain what the scan cannot reassess.”

Look for traceable placements, comparable area scope, explicit uncertainty, preserved identities and honest
relationship semantics. A convincing diagram with invented transitions is a failure; an unchanged landscape
with well-placed new evidence can be a successful update.
